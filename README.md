## The entry

> it's you from 2037. stop texting 'five minutes away' from your couch. your friends have a group chat called 'five minutes' now. you're not in it.

## Why this one wins

Everybody has sent that text: "five minutes away," typed from the couch, shoes still off. This entry takes that one universal little lie and follows it eleven years into the future, where it has grown into something worse than being late — it has become your reputation. Your friends have a group chat about it. It has a name. And the deadpan final beat, "you're not in it," is the whole joke landing in four words: the future self isn't warning you about a disaster, he's telling you that you already became the punchline.

The voice is exactly how a future self would actually text: lowercase, blunt, no greeting, no small talk, straight to the embarrassing part. It needs zero setup and zero context — it reads like a real message that just buzzed in your pocket. And in a field of 500+ entries full of dying houseplants, sourdough starters named Gerald, and unfinished online courses, the "five minutes away" lie is untouched territory: instantly relatable, completely fresh, and funny on first read.

## Run it

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This writes the entry line to `out/entry.txt` (single line, under 200 characters, no trailing newline), reads it back to verify, and prints its sha256. Output is deterministic: every run produces byte-identical results.
